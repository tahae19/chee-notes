2Extremum Principle

An extremum principle predicts equilibrium by identifying a variable or function that is driven to a maximum or a minimum.

Extremum principle: a statement that the equilibrium state is found by maximizing or minimizing an appropriate quantity, subject to the constraints.

4Potential, Force & Equilibrium

A mechanical potential is related to the force by the negative derivative:

The minus sign means the force points in the direction that lowers the potential energy. Without that sign, the model would predict the ball being pushed uphill.

Thus mechanical equilibrium can be described in two equivalent ways in this one-dimensional example: as an extremum of the potential energy, or as the point where the net force is zero.

Exam
Do not lose the minus sign in the force–potential relation. The instructor singled it out as something to “never forget.”
Common Mistake
A zero first derivative only identifies a stationary point. It does not by itself tell you whether the point is stable or unstable. Use the shape of the potential or the second derivative.

Friction and settling

The class used the rolling ball to emphasize that, without friction, kinetic and potential energy would keep exchanging and the ball would continue to oscillate. With friction, the motion dies out and the ball settles near the potential minimum.

Thermodynamic analogy

The instructor used this mechanical picture to motivate later thermodynamic “forces,” especially pressure and chemical potential. The exact thermodynamic derivative identities were not developed in this lecture.

5Types of Mechanical Equilibrium

Original schematic summary of the four equilibrium types discussed in L03.

5.1 Stable equilibrium

A small displacement raises the potential, so the system tends to return to the minimum.

5.2 Neutral / indifferent equilibrium

The potential is flat. There is no preferred position along the flat direction. The instructor called this “indifferent equilibrium”; the textbook uses “neutral equilibrium.”

5.3 Metastable equilibrium

A metastable state is a local minimum, but a deeper minimum exists elsewhere. The eraser demonstration showed that a small tilt returns to the original state, while a larger tilt crosses the barrier and reaches another state.

5.4 Unstable state

At a maximum, even a small perturbation moves the system away from the stationary point.

6Degree of Freedom & Constraint

Degree of freedom: a variable the system is allowed to change by itself.
Constraint: a variable imposed or fixed from outside; the system is not free to change it.

For the ball in the trough, x is a degree of freedom. If the ball were fixed at a specified position, that position would instead be a constraint.

For the pressure lattice example later in the lecture, the number of particles is held fixed while the number of accessible lattice sites changes:

Therefore N is a constraint and M is the degree of freedom in that model.

Important
“Degree of freedom” and “constraint” are not permanent labels attached to a physical variable. They depend on what the experiment or model allows to change and what is held fixed.

The instructor also noted that degree of freedom will matter later in the Gibbs phase rule.

درجه‌ی آزادی و قید

درجه‌ی آزادی متغیری است که سیستم اجازه دارد خودش تغییر دهد. قید متغیری است که از بیرون ثابت شده است.

در مثال گاز شبکه‌ای، تعداد ذرات ثابت نگه داشته می‌شود اما تعداد جایگاه‌های قابل دسترس می‌تواند تغییر کند. پس تعداد ذرات قید و اندازه‌ی فضای قابل دسترس درجه‌ی آزادی است.

یک کمیت ممکن است در یک مسئله درجه‌ی آزادی و در مسئله‌ای دیگر قید باشد.

7Maximum Multiplicity as an Extremum Principle

The lecture now shifts from a mechanical system with one or a few bodies to statistical systems with enormous numbers of particles.

Multiplicity W: the number of distinguishable microscopic arrangements associated with a specified composition or macrostate.

The central principle introduced in L03 is:

For equally probable microstates, a macrostate with more microstates is more probable because there are more ways to realize it.

Important
This is the central conceptual jump of L03. Random microscopic behavior can produce sharply predictable macroscopic behavior when the number of particles or trials becomes very large.

13Microstate & Macrostate

Macrostate: a state specified by a macroscopic, observable, or controllable variable.
Microstate: one detailed microscopic configuration of all the elements in the system.

For the polymer example:

The lecture ends by generalizing this idea to the air in the classroom. A macrostate can be described by macroscopic quantities such as approximately one atmosphere of pressure and about 22–23 °C. A microstate would require the position and velocity of every individual molecule.

Exam
Be able to say this in words: a macrostate is the coarse, measurable description; a microstate is one complete microscopic arrangement; multiplicity counts how many microstates correspond to the macrostate.

روابط کلیدی

روابط بالا همان معادلاتی هستند که باید برای مرور سریع این جلسه در یک صفحه جلوی چشم باشند.

مهم‌تر از حفظ کردن جداگانه‌ی آن‌ها، دانستن نقش هر رابطه در زنجیره‌ی استدلال جلسه است: کمینه‌ی انرژی در مکانیک و بیشینه‌ی چندگانگی در سامانه‌های آماری.

17What I Need to Know for the Exam

  1. Define an extremum principle and explain how it predicts equilibrium.
  2. Derive the equilibrium position for the quadratic valley from z = x² and V = mgz.
  3. Use and interpret F = -dV/dx, including the meaning of the minus sign.
  4. Distinguish stable, neutral, metastable, and unstable equilibrium from the potential-energy curve.
  5. Use the first- and second-derivative tests correctly.
  6. Define degree of freedom and constraint, and identify them in the lecture examples.
  7. Define multiplicity W in words.
  8. Calculate W for a two-category composition using factorials.
  9. Explain why 2H/2T is the maximum-multiplicity composition for four fair coin tosses.
  10. Explain qualitatively why the dominant composition becomes overwhelmingly favored as N becomes very large.
  11. Use W(N,M) for the lattice-gas pressure example and reproduce W = 1, 4, 10.
  12. Compute the corresponding pressure-example probabilities and check that they sum to 1.
  13. Reproduce the mixing multiplicities 1 → 16 → 36 and explain why uniform mixing is favored.
  14. Reproduce the polymer multiplicities W(1) = 2, W(2) = 4, W(3) = 1 and explain elastic retraction.
  15. Distinguish a microstate from a macrostate.
  16. State the central relation between multiplicity and macrostate probability: more microstates means a more probable macrostate when microstates are equally probable.
  17. Recognize S = k_B ln W as the next-step entropy relation, while remembering that its derivation belongs to the next lecture.
  18. Always include units and perform simple consistency checks.
Exam
If you can explain the three model examples—pressure, mixing, and rubber—using the same sentence “the system moves toward the macrostate with greater multiplicity,” you understand the core of L03.
