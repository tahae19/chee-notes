2. Conditional Probability & Degree of Correlation Back to TOC

2.1 Conditional Probability

The conditional probability P(B|A) is the probability that B occurs when A is already known to have occurred.

P(AB)=P(B|A)P(A)

This is the general multiplication rule for two events.

احتمال شرطی یعنی وقتی از قبل می‌دانیم یک رویداد رخ داده است، احتمال رویداد دوم را در همان شرایط بررسی کنیم.

2.2 Independent Events

If A does not affect B, the events are independent:

P(B|A)=P(B)

Therefore the joint probability becomes:

P(AB)=P(A)P(B)

2.3 Degree of Correlation

The degree of correlation measures how strongly knowing A changes the probability of B.

g=P(B|A)P(B)

Using the general multiplication rule:

g=P(AB)P(A)P(B)
ValueInterpretation
g = 1Independent / uncorrelated.
g > 1Positively correlated: A increases the probability of B.
g < 1Negatively correlated: A decreases the probability of B.
g = 0If A occurs, B does not occur.
Transcript Correction

The transcript garbles the denominator of the correlation expression. The correct relation used in the textbook and intended in the lecture is g = P(B|A) / P(B), not division by P(A).

Professor Example - Clouds and Rain

Assume:

P(rain|clouds)=0.5
P(rain)=0.1
g=0.50.1=5

Clouds are strongly positively correlated with rain in this example.

در مثال استاد، احتمال عادی باران کم است، اما وقتی وجود ابر را می‌دانیم این احتمال چند برابر می‌شود. بنابراین بین این دو رویداد همبستگی مثبت وجود دارد.
Important

Correlation does not prove causation. The professor emphasized that correlation is a starting point for investigating a causal relationship, not proof of one.

3. Combinatorics: Sequence vs Composition Back to TOC

Combinatorics provides methods for counting the outcomes of composite events. In this course, the key distinction is between an exact sequence and a composition.

3.1 Exact Sequence

For four fair coin tosses, consider the exact sequence:

HHTH

Because each toss is independent and has probability one-half:

P(HHTH)=(12)4=116
Transcript Correction

The transcript drops one factor of one-half in this example. Four tosses require four probability factors, so the exact sequence probability is 1/16.

3.2 Composition

Now ignore the exact order and ask for the composition “three heads and one tail.” Four exact sequences have this composition:

THHH
HTHH
HHTH
HHHT

Therefore the probability of this composition is:

P(3H,1T)=4(12)4=14
Transcript Correction

The lecture transcript sometimes calls these “four compositions.” Using the textbook terminology precisely, there is one composition (3H, 1T) and four sequences that realize it.

تفاوت اصلی این است که در دنباله، ترتیب دقیق اهمیت دارد. در ترکیب فقط تعداد هر نوع نتیجه مهم است و ترتیب نادیده گرفته می‌شود.
Exam

Before using any counting formula, decide whether the problem asks for one exact sequence or for all sequences having a specified composition.

4. Factorial & Permutations Back to TOC

4.1 Four Distinguishable Letters

For four different letters W, X, Y, and Z used once each:

4×3×2×1=24

The compact notation is:

4!=24

4.2 General Definition

N!=N(N−1)(N−2)⋯3·2·1
0!=1
فاکتوریل تعداد ترتیب‌های ممکن برای مجموعه‌ای از اشیای قابل تشخیص را به‌صورت فشرده نشان می‌دهد. مقدار فاکتوریل صفر طبق تعریف برابر یک است و در محاسبات این درس بارها ظاهر می‌شود.

4.3 Twenty-Six Different Letters

If all 26 letters are used exactly once, the number of sequences is:

26!≈4.03×1026

If repetition is allowed independently at every one of the 26 positions, the number of sequences is:

2626
Remember

More constraints mean fewer possible outcomes. “Use every letter once” is a constraint, so 26! must be smaller than 2626.

5. Indistinguishable Objects & Multiplicity Back to TOC

5.1 Example: A, A, H

If the two A's were distinguishable, they could be labeled A1 and A2, giving 3! arrangements. But when the A's are indistinguishable, swapping them does not create a new distinguishable sequence.

W=3!2!=3

The three distinguishable sequences are:

HAA
AHA
AAH
وقتی دو شیء یکسان هستند، جابه‌جا کردن آن‌ها حالت جدید قابل تشخیصی نمی‌سازد. بنابراین شمارش فاکتوریلی اولیه بیش‌شماری می‌کند و باید آن را اصلاح کنیم.

5.2 Example: CHEESE

CHEESE has six letters, with three indistinguishable E's:

W=6!3!=120

5.3 Example: FREEZER

FREEZER has seven letters, with three E's and two R's:

W=7!3!2!=420
Transcript Correction

The transcript is garbled in this example and briefly sounds like two factors of 3!. The textbook example corresponding to the professor's calculation is FREEZER, so the correct denominator is 3! × 2!, giving 420.

5.4 General Multiplicity Formula

For N total objects distributed among t categories, where category j contains nj indistinguishable objects:

∑tj=1nj=N
W=N!n1!n2!⋯nt!
Important

The professor emphasized that the hardest part of combinatorics is often not the arithmetic. It is defining the outcomes and categories correctly.

6. Two-Category Multiplicity & Poker Back to TOC

6.1 Two Categories

If there are only two categories, n objects are in the selected category and N − n are in the other category:

W(n,N)=C(N,n)=N!n!(N−n)!

This is read as “N choose n.”

Example - Three Heads and One Tail
W=4!3!1!=4

6.2 Poker Hand Counting

A standard deck contains 52 cards. A classical poker hand contains 5 cards. The order in which the five cards are drawn does not matter, so the number of distinguishable hands is:

W=52!5!47!=2,598,960

There are four royal flushes, one for each suit. Therefore:

P(royal flush)=42,598,960≈1.54×10−6
Transcript Correction

The professor rounded the number of poker hands to “about two million,” leading to the rough statement “about one in 500,000.” The exact count is 2,598,960 hands, so the royal-flush probability is approximately 1.54 × 10−6.

در مسائل انتخاب بدون توجه به ترتیب، ابتدا باید تشخیص دهیم که جابه‌جایی اعضای انتخاب‌شده نتیجه‌ی جدیدی ایجاد نمی‌کند. به همین دلیل در مثال دست پوکر، ترتیب پنج کارت در شمارش نهایی حذف می‌شود.

7. Binomial Distribution Back to TOC

7.1 Two Outcomes

Assume each independent trial has two mutually exclusive outcomes. Let the selected outcome have probability p. The other outcome has probability 1 − p.

7.2 Two Trials: Build the Distribution

SequenceProbability
AAp2
ABp(1 − p)
BA(1 − p)p
BB(1 − p)2

Because these four possibilities are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive, their probabilities must add to one:

p2+2p(1−p)+(1−p)2=[p+(1−p)]2=1
Important

The professor repeatedly emphasized: do not merely say “the probabilities must add to one.” Calculate and check that they actually do.

7.3 General Derivation

We now ask: what is the probability of exactly n selected outcomes in N independent trials?

Step 1 - Probability of one particular sequence

pn(1−p)N−n

Step 2 - Number of sequences with that composition

W(n,N)=N!n!(N−n)!

Step 3 - Multiply them

P(n,N)=pn(1−p)N−nN!n!(N−n)!
Exam

Understand the formula as two pieces:

  1. pn(1 − p)N−n: probability of one particular sequence.
  2. N!/[n!(N−n)!]: number of sequences having the required composition.
فرمول دوجمله‌ای را فقط حفظ نکن. ابتدا احتمال یک ترتیب مشخص را حساب می‌کنیم و سپس آن را در تعداد ترتیب‌هایی که همان ترکیب را می‌سازند ضرب می‌کنیم. این منطق، خود فرمول را قابل فهم می‌کند.

10. Continuous Distributions & Normalization Back to TOC

10.1 Probability Density

When x is continuous, p(x) is a probability density. The probability of finding x in a small interval from x to x + dx is:

p(x)dx

For the range used in the lecture:

∫∞0p(x)dx=1
Transcript Clarification

For a continuous variable, p(x) itself is a density; the probability over an infinitesimal interval is p(x)dx.

10.2 Non-Normalized Distribution

If g(x) is not normalized, first calculate its normalization integral:

g0=∫bag(x)dx

Then define the normalized probability density:

p(x)=g(x)g0

10.3 Exponential Example

The professor used:

g(x)=e−ax, x≥0

For a > 0:

g0=∫∞0e−axdx=1a

Therefore:

p(x)=ae−ax

12. Large-N Behavior & Bridge to Entropy Back to TOC

The professor ended the lecture by asking what happens as the number of trials becomes much larger: 10, 100, 1000, and beyond.

For a fair two-outcome process, the distribution becomes increasingly concentrated around an equal fraction of the two outcomes. The most probable composition occurs near:

n≈N2

This is the conceptual bridge to statistical thermodynamics: when N is very large, the overwhelming number of microscopic sequences lie near the most probable macroscopic composition.

Transcript Clarification

The transcript says that for very large N the single equal-heads/equal-tails outcome “becomes the only one that happens.” The textbook's more precise statement is that the distribution becomes sharply concentrated around the most probable composition. It is the relative composition that becomes highly predictable; the probability of one exact composition should not be interpreted as literally becoming 1.

وقتی تعداد آزمایش‌ها بسیار زیاد می‌شود، نسبت نتایج به مقدار محتمل‌تر نزدیک می‌شود و نوسان نسبی کوچک‌تر می‌گردد. این همان ایده‌ای است که بعداً برای توضیح رفتار ماکروسکوپی تعداد بسیار زیادی مولکول استفاده می‌شود.
Remember

The lecture's destination is the idea of a most probable macrostate arising from multiplicity. Entropy will quantify this idea in the following development of the course.

13. Professor Emphasis & Common Mistakes Back to TOC

Professor Emphasis

  • Read the book and follow the notes; the concepts build on each other.
  • Always check calculations instead of assuming the answer “must” be correct.
  • Have an estimate or expectation before trusting a calculator result.
  • In combinatorics, define the outcomes and categories before choosing a formula.
  • Ask exactly what probability question is being asked.
  • Correlation is not the same as causation.

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing one exact sequence with a composition.
  • Using N! when some objects are indistinguishable.
  • Forgetting the combinatorial factor in a binomial probability.
  • Using P(A) instead of P(B) in the denominator of the correlation ratio.
  • Forgetting that 0! = 1.
  • Treating p(x) as a point probability rather than a probability density.
  • Failing to verify that a probability distribution is normalized.
Common Mistake

Do not jump straight to a factorial formula. First answer: What are the trials? What are the categories? Does order matter? Which objects are distinguishable?

روش حل خوب در این جلسه از تشخیص درست صورت مسئله شروع می‌شود. اگر نوع رویداد و دسته‌بندی‌ها درست تعیین شوند، بخش محاسباتی معمولاً ساده‌تر می‌شود.
